# Communication & Consultation Strategy

## 1. Core Narrative and Framing

All communication uses a trauma‑informed, non‑stigmatising narrative:

- **Name it carefully:** “The socioeconomic and life‑safety impacts of targeted hostility”, not a deficit list.
- **Define the chain:** We examine how systemic protection failures *cause* job loss, eviction, forced displacement, and suicidality.
- **Intersectionality:** Explicitly centre Black, trans, disabled, and migrant experiences.
- **No victim‑blaming:** Never say “LGBTQ+ people are more likely to become homeless”. Say “hostility, compounded by systemic failures, drives risk of homelessness.”

## 3. Phased Consultation Method

### Phase A – Confidential Pre‑Consultation with Lived Experience
- Paid, closed focus groups and 1:1 interviews.
- Recruited via trusted community organisations and diaspora peer networks (e.g. closed Brazilian LGBTQ+ groups).
- Ensure representation of those who lost jobs/housing, left the country, or navigated with different legal statuses.
- Bilingual, trauma‑informed community researchers; clinical support available.

### Phase B – Bilateral Briefing Meetings
- One‑hour confidential meetings with policy leads of key organisations.
- Present a two‑page concept note; ask about casework data, safe consultation methods, and willingness to join an advisory panel.
- Transnational additions: dedicated meetings with UNHCR, IOM, Brazilian human rights bodies, consular services.

### Phase C – Thematic Roundtables (Chatham House Rule)
1. **“From Harassment to Homelessness”** – domestic service providers and police.
2. **“The Transnational Displacement Corridor: The Brazilian Case Study”** – immigration lawyers, Brazilian NGOs, Portuguese diaspora, UN observer.
3. **“Forced Emigration and Suicidality”** – mental health charities, ILGA World, IASP.

### Phase D – Formal Advisory Group (Co‑Design)
- 12–15 members: IE, NI, GB orgs equally weighted; 2+ lived‑experience seats (paid); 1 Global South diaspora seat; 1 international human rights body seat; academic partner.

## 4. Deep‑Dive Hooks: The Brazilian / EEA / Citizenship Dynamic

- **Legal liminality:** Map the vulnerability of those awaiting naturalisation while facing escalating hostility. Engage INIS and Home Office discretely.
- **EEA passport portability failure:** A Brazilian with an Italian passport fleeing to Italy may fall through all cracks. Consult Arcigay, ILGA Portugal, TGEU.
- **Return after Irish citizenship:** Dual nationals returning to Brazil have consular access but no guaranteed safety. Raise with Department of Foreign Affairs and feed into EU consular protection guidelines.
- **The asylum black hole:** Refugees granted status based on SOGI in Brazil then harassed in the host country. Explore with UNHCR whether this triggers new protection obligations.

---

## 5. Ethical and Strategic Safeguards

- **Data sensitivity:** No database subject to Hostile Environment or immigration enforcement. Anonymised case studies only.
- **Precarious migrant protection:** Community‑based consent advocates; clear non‑disclosure guarantees.
- **Capacity support:** Stipends for small, volunteer‑run organisations to prevent exploitation.
