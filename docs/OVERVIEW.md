# The Trasnú Project – Overview

**Full title:** From Targeted Hostility to Transnational Displacement: Tracing the Socioeconomic and Life‑Safety Consequences for LGBTQ+ People in Ireland, Northern Ireland, Great Britain, and Beyond

## The Problem

LGBTQ+ people in Ireland, Northern Ireland, and Great Britain experience harassment, stalking, and physical assault at rates far exceeding the general population. While the immediate trauma is recognised, the cascading consequences are not. This project addresses a critical, underexamined causal chain: how that targeted hostility drives **unemployment**, **homelessness**, **forced emigration** (including coerced return to a country of origin), and **suicide**.

## The Unique Lens

The project expands the geographic frame beyond the three initial jurisdictions. It recognises that the decision to leave—or the feeling of having no choice but to leave—is profoundly shaped by a person’s legal status:

- Non‑EEA citizenship (e.g. Brazilian)
- EEA/EU passport (e.g. Italian, Portuguese) without host‑country nationality
- Irish or British citizenship (recent or long‑held, often dual)

A person’s status bundle dictates *where* they can go, what safety nets exist, and the risk they face upon arrival. The project uses the **Brazilian diaspora** as a central case study to map this transnational displacement corridor.

## Core Aims

1. **Map the causal pathways** from targeted hostility to life‑shattering outcomes.
2. **Develop ethical, community‑led consultation methods** that protect precarious participants.
3. **Produce actionable policy blueprints** for governments, police, housing authorities, and international bodies.
4. **Create a replicable framework** for other transnational corridors (e.g. Nigeria‑UK, Pakistan‑Ireland).

## Jurisdictional Scope

| Layer | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Ireland (IE) | EU member state with developing hate crime legislation. |
| Northern Ireland (NI) | UK region with devolved justice and distinct post‑conflict dynamics. |
| Great Britain (GB) | Three legal systems (England, Scotland, Wales), all with specific hate crime and housing law. |
| Global | Countries of origin/destination (e.g. Brazil), EU institutions, international human rights bodies. |

## Why Now

- The EU LGBTIQ Strategy is under renewal.
- The UK’s hostile environment policies and Ireland’s immigration reforms create new vulnerabilities.
- The UN Independent Expert on SOGI has flagged forced displacement as a priority theme.
- Survivors and frontline organisations are crying out for systemic, cross‑border solutions.

This project turns lived experience into leverage for change.
