# Roadmap

## Phase 1: Foundation (Months 1–6)

- [ ] Secure academic host and full ethics approval
- [ ] Recruit core advisory group (IE, NI, GB, Brazilian diaspora)
- [ ] Conduct lived‑experience focus groups and develop composite personas
- [ ] Publish research ethics protocol and data protection toolkit
- [ ] Hold first transnational corridor roundtable (Brazilian case study)
- [ ] Complete bilateral meetings with anchor organisations

## Phase 2: Systematic Data Collection (Months 7–15)

- [ ] Conduct 40+ practitioner interviews across housing, policing, immigration law
- [ ] Deploy anonymised online practitioner survey (UK/IE/NI)
- [ ] Map policy gaps and produce interim working paper
- [ ] Second thematic roundtable: “From Hostility to Homelessness”
- [ ] Third thematic roundtable: “Forced Emigration and Suicidality”

## Phase 3: Analysis and Outputs (Months 16–24)

- [ ] Launch final report with jurisdiction‑specific policy recommendations
- [ ] Submit evidence to UN Universal Periodic Review (Ireland, UK, Brazil)
- [ ] Request thematic hearing before Inter‑American Commission on Human Rights
- [ ] Present at ILGA World Conference and EU Fundamental Rights Agency events
- [ ] Release open‑access policy briefs and the transnational pathway mapping tool
- [ ] Publish journal articles on methodology and findings

## Phase 4: Growth and Replication (Year 3 onwards)

- [ ] Adapt methodology for Nigeria–UK/Ireland and Pakistan–UK corridors
- [ ] Develop a secure, anonymised digital storytelling platform for survivor narratives (with robust content moderation)
- [ ] Explore AI‑driven risk modelling for forced returnees, contingent on rigorous ethical assessment
- [ ] Establish a permanent “Queer Displacement Observatory” hosted by a university consortium
- [ ] Train community organisations in the replication framework
- [ ] Advocate for a cross‑governmental taskforce on LGBTQ+ transnational displacement
