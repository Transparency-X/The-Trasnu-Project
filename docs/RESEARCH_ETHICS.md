# Research Ethics & Data Protection Protocol

## 1. Core Principles

- **Do no harm:** The safety of participants – especially those with precarious immigration status, active suicidality, or fear of being outed – is paramount.
- **Lived‑experience leadership:** Survivors and directly impacted individuals co‑design every stage.
- **Confidentiality over visibility:** Data will never be shared in a way that could identify an individual or be used against them by state authorities.

## 2. Informed Consent

- **Layered consent process:** Participants agree separately to being interviewed, recorded, quoted (anonymously), and included in research outputs. Consent can be withdrawn at any point without penalty.
- **Language access:** Consent forms and discussions available in English, Portuguese, and other languages as required. Bilingual advocates present.
- **Third‑party consent:** For focus groups, participants collectively agree to group confidentiality terms.
- **No immigration data:** We do not collect, record, or store any information about a person’s immigration application number, Home Office/INIS reference, or passport details.

## 3. Anonymisation and Data Storage

- **All data pseudonymised** at the point of collection. Direct identifiers (names, specific addresses) are never stored digitally with interview content.
- **Data stored** on encrypted, university‑approved servers with two‑factor authentication. No cloud storage of raw data unless end‑to‑end encrypted and hosted within the EU/EEA.
- **Minimum data retention:** Raw audio/video files destroyed immediately after verified transcription and member‑checking. Transcripts kept for a maximum of five years post‑project, then securely deleted.
- **No database linkage:** We do not build a central database that could be subject to UK Hostile Environment measures or Irish immigration enforcement.

## 4. Participant Protections for Precarious Status Individuals

- All recruitment is done via trusted community gatekeepers. We never approach potential participants directly without a warm introduction.
- Interviewers are trained to recognise and gently steer away from any disclosure that might create legal risk for the participant. If a participant inadvertently reveals undocumented status, that information is **not recorded** and the interviewer makes a contemporaneous note to destroy any related content.
- Participants are provided with a clear “What we are legally required to report” sheet. In most jurisdictions this is limited to immediate risk of serious harm to self or others, or child safeguarding concerns. There is **no obligation** to report immigration status to authorities, and we explicitly state that we will not do so.
- Each participant is offered a referral to a specialist LGBTQ+ immigration solicitor (pro bono partnership) for any advice they might need, entirely separate from the research.

## 5. Mandatory Reporting Boundaries

- The project team holds a detailed mapping of mandatory reporting duties in each jurisdiction (IE, NI, England, Scotland, Wales). The limits of confidentiality are clearly discussed before any interview.
- If a participant discloses ongoing abuse of a child or vulnerable adult, or an imminent, credible threat to life, the team will follow the university’s safeguarding protocol, which involves a designated safeguarding officer (not law enforcement) making a risk assessment. The participant will be informed at every step.

## 6. Compensation and Avoiding Exploitation

- All lived‑experience advisors and focus group members are paid fairly for their time, at rates benchmarked against national living wage standards.
- Support is offered before, during, and after participation: access to a trauma‑informed counsellor, option to have a trusted friend present, and a post‑session check‑in.

## 7. Ethical Review

- This protocol must be approved by the host university’s Research Ethics Committee before any data collection begins.
- Any substantive changes must be re‑approved and communicated to the Advisory Group.

We hold ourselves accountable to the communities we serve, not just to institutional ethics boards. If you believe we have fallen short, please see our [Code of Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) for reporting mechanisms.
