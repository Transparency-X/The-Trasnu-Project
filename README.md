# The Trasnú Project

**Tracking Queer Displacement and Survival Across Borders**

[![Project Status: Active](https://img.shields.io/badge/status-active-brightgreen)]()
[![License: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0](https://img.shields.io/badge/license-CC%20BY--NC--SA%204.0-lightgrey)]()

**Trasnú** (Irish for “crossing”) is a transnational research and advocacy initiative investigating the cascading consequences of anti-LGBTQ+ harassment, stalking, and assault in Ireland, Northern Ireland, and Great Britain. We map how targeted hostility forces individuals into unemployment, homelessness, coerced emigration, and suicidal crisis—with a unique focus on the legal status pathways that determine *where* people are forced to go, and what risks they face when they get there.

## Overview

LGBTQ+ people in Ireland, Northern Ireland, and Great Britain experience targeted hostility at alarming rates. The immediate trauma is well-documented; what is not is the full downstream chain of harm: job loss, eviction, destitution, and the gut-wrenching decision to leave—sometimes to a country where a person’s sexuality or gender identity places them in mortal danger.

The Trasnú Project connects the dots. We examine how a report of stalking in Belfast can lead to a Brazilian trans woman, newly naturalised as Irish, feeling forced to return to São Paulo. We trace the legal liminalities—non-EEA citizenship, EU passports, dual nationality—that transform a personal crisis into a transnational protection failure.

This repository serves as the central knowledge hub for our methodology, ethical protocols, consultation frameworks, and evolving evidence base.

---

## Why This Matters

- **Legal blind spots:** No jurisdiction tracks what happens to an LGBTQ+ person who flees the UK/Ireland after hate crime victimisation.
- **Intersectional invisibility:** A Black queer migrant with a Portuguese passport and a pending Irish citizenship application occupies a policy void exploited by systems failure.
- **Global obligations:** Under international human rights law, states must protect against non-state persecution; failing to prevent forced displacement into unsafe conditions constitutes a secondary rights violation.

---

## Core Aims

1. **Map the causal pathways** from targeted hostility to unemployment, homelessness, forced emigration, and suicide.
2. **Develop ethical, community-led consultation methodologies** for engaging survivors, especially those with precarious immigration status.
3. **Produce actionable policy blueprints** for governments, police, housing authorities, and international bodies.
4. **Create a replicable framework** for other transnational corridors (e.g., Nigerian, Filipino, Ukrainian diaspora routes).

Features (from docs/FEATURES.md)

Feature Description
Multi-jurisdictional consultation framework A phased, trauma-informed blueprint for engaging stakeholders across IE, NI, GB, and global bodies.
Lived-experience co-design model Compensated, confidential panels ensuring survivors shape every research question and output.
Transnational displacement pathway mapping A conceptual tool to visualise how legal status (non-EEA, EEA, dual citizen) determines forced migration routes and destination risks.
Composite persona methodology Ethically safe, evidence-based composite cases (e.g., “Rafael, Carla, Luana”) to guide policy discussions without exposing individuals.
Practitioner wisdom interview protocol A standardised instrument to gather anonymised case insights from frontline workers in housing, policing, and migrant support.
Policy gap analysis matrix A cross-referenced assessment of where IE, NI, GB, and international law fail to protect against displacement-inducing hostility.
Ethical data protection toolkit A ready-to-deploy set of consent forms, data storage protocols, and mandatory reporting guidelines for research with precarious populations.
Global advocacy submission pack Templated UPR, IACHR, and UN Special Procedures briefs to streamline evidence submission.

Roadmap (from docs/ROADMAP.md)

Phase 1: Foundation (Months 1-6)

· Establish academic host and ethics approval
· Recruit core advisory group (IE, NI, GB, Brazilian diaspora)
· Complete lived-experience focus groups and composite persona development
· Publish ethics protocol and data protection toolkit
· Hold first transnational corridor roundtable (Brazilian case study)

Phase 2: Systematic Data Collection (Months 7-15)

· Conduct 40+ practitioner interviews across housing, policing, immigration law
· Deploy online practitioner survey (anonymised, UK/IE/NI)
· Map policy gaps and produce interim working paper
· Second thematic roundtable: “From Hostility to Homelessness”

Phase 3: Analysis and Outputs (Months 16-24)

· Launch final report with policy recommendations
· Submit evidence to UN Universal Periodic Review (Ireland, UK, Brazil)
· Present at ILGA World Conference and EU Fundamental Rights Agency event
· Release open-access policy briefs and transnational pathway mapping tool

Phase 4: Growth and Replication (Year 3+)

· Adapt methodology for Nigeria–UK/Ireland and Pakistan–UK corridors
· Develop a secure, anonymised digital storytelling platform for survivor narratives
· Explore AI-driven risk modelling for forced returnees (ethics permitting)
· Establish permanent “Queer Displacement Observatory” hosted by a university consortium
